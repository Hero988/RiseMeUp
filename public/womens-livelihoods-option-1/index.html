Women and girls at the heart of the project

A place to regain strength.
A future shaped by possibility.

Virgin Mary Farm’s central purpose is to support women affected by Sudan’s war, including survivors of sexual violence, women whose husbands have been killed, and mothers raising children alone. Our planned work brings together sustainable farming education, livelihood opportunities and community connection for women from the Coptic Orthodox and Nuba Mountains communities.

Dignity and independence

Every woman and girl deserves to live with dignity, feel valued and have opportunities to learn. Iman’s vision brings together education, practical training and opportunities for women to earn an income, support their families and shape their own futures. A supportive community can help rebuild confidence, independence and hope. The farm is not yet operational, and this describes the programme we aim to develop with communities and partners.

Opportunity for girls

For girls and young women, we plan education and skills opportunities that build confidence and widen future choices. Girls’ learning will be age-appropriate, supervised and designed to support their safety and continued schooling.

Developing this responsibly

Women’s voices, privacy and voluntary participation should guide the design. Access should not require disclosure of violence or trauma. Consultation should address caring responsibilities, travel and accessibility. We invite women’s organisations and qualified psychosocial partners to help develop appropriate support and referral arrangements; these services are not yet established. Farming is not a substitute for specialist care.

Training for everyone who works at the farm

Our commitment is that everyone working at Virgin Mary Farm will receive training appropriate to their role. The planned programme will cover sustainable farming, water management, safe equipment use, maintenance and practical skills for earning a living, with induction and continued learning as the farm develops.

Dr. Eng. Khalid Salih brings university teaching experience from Berlin and expertise in renewable energy and workforce development. Our training plan will draw on his experience and the practical learning approach of the DIESALIHS Grand Renaissance Academy, alongside agricultural specialists and equipment suppliers. Course content, instructors and delivery arrangements will be developed as the farm is rebuilt.
