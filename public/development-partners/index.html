Women and girls at the heart of the project

Dignity. Independence.
Hope for a new beginning.

Virgin Mary Farm’s central purpose is to support women affected by Sudan’s war, including survivors of sexual violence, women whose husbands have been killed, and mothers raising children alone. Our planned work brings together sustainable farming education, livelihood opportunities and community connection for women from the Coptic Orthodox and Nuba Mountains communities.

Every woman and girl deserves to live with dignity, feel valued and have opportunities to learn. Iman’s vision brings together education, practical training and opportunities for women to earn an income, support their families and shape their own futures. A supportive community can help rebuild confidence, independence and hope. The farm is not yet operational, and this describes the programme we aim to develop with communities and partners.

For girls and young women, we plan education and skills opportunities that build confidence and widen future choices. Girls’ learning will be age-appropriate, supervised and designed to support their safety and continued schooling.

Developing this responsibly

Women’s voices, privacy and voluntary participation should guide the design. Access should not require disclosure of violence or trauma. Consultation should address caring responsibilities, travel and accessibility. We invite women’s organisations and qualified psychosocial partners to help develop appropriate support and referral arrangements; these services are not yet established. Farming is not a substitute for specialist care.

Training for everyone who works at the farm

Our commitment is that everyone working at Virgin Mary Farm will receive training appropriate to their role. The planned programme will cover sustainable farming, water management, safe equipment use, maintenance and practical skills for earning a living, with induction and continued learning as the farm develops.

Dr. Eng. Khalid Salih brings university teaching experience from Berlin and expertise in renewable energy and workforce development. Our training plan will draw on his experience and the practical learning approach of the DIESALIHS Grand Renaissance Academy, alongside agricultural specialists and equipment suppliers. Course content, instructors and delivery arrangements will be developed as the farm is rebuilt.

02 / A first stage to develop together

Restore water.
Start growing. Share skills.

A smaller first stage makes the next decisions concrete. This is a proposed scope for technical assessment, not an approved engineering design or a funded delivery programme.

Proposed scope

Assess the seven destroyed wells. Subject to technical findings, establish an initial water source through well rehabilitation or replacement, build new water storage, and install a complete pumping and irrigation system for an initial cultivation and training area. The growing area, crop plan and participant numbers will follow the water assessment and community consultation.

  1. 01

    Confirm the foundations

    Agree land-use terms, the legal project recipient and local responsibilities; confirm safe access; assess the seven destroyed wells and options for rehabilitation or replacement, including water availability and suitability.

    Evidence to proceed: dated assessment, well records and agreed responsibilities.

  2. 02

    Design and cost the first system

    Agree a viable water source, define the growing area, prepare well-restoration, new water-storage, pump and irrigation specifications, obtain comparable quotations and agree community participation and asset-custody arrangements.

    Evidence to proceed: technical design, itemised budget, operating plan and funding agreement.

  3. 03

    Install, test and hand over

    Once funding, access and secure storage are confirmed, procure compatible equipment, install the system, test its performance and train the named operators.

    Evidence to proceed: commissioning results, asset register, operator handover and maintenance schedule.

  4. 04

    Grow, teach and review

    Begin a supervised growing cycle and sustainable farming education. Record water use, crop performance, participation and costs, then decide with communities and partners whether to expand.

    Evidence for expansion: first-cycle review and an updated, costed plan.

Timing depends on safe access, assessment findings, written agreements, available funding and procurement. Delivery dates will be agreed after these dependencies are resolved.

The full Virgin Mary Farm vision

A long-term ambition.
A practical first step.

The wider project retains ten planned greenhouses, biogas, poultry, produce sorting and packaging, and sustainable farming education. The Mango Project remains a supporting subproject within Virgin Mary Farm.

The proposal dated 19 November 2018 is historical background. Its four facility phases and the newer land-development concept describe long-term ambitions; they are not the immediate post-war delivery sequence or a current budget.

Our newer five-phase recovery roadmap proposes gradual development across the 130-hectare site, with a long-term ambition to reach 300+ families. Areas and participant numbers are indicative; available surplus could contribute to expansion, alongside further partner support.

01 / Participation

Fair access to opportunity

Proposed selection criteria will consider household need, displacement, barriers to livelihoods and interest in farming or training. Criteria should be shared locally and applied transparently, with a way to raise concerns. Participation should not depend on religious observance.

02 / Community voice

Shape the project together

The proposed consultation process will include both the Coptic Orthodox and Nuba Mountains communities, with women as a priority, alongside young people and displaced families, helping to shape training, schedules and access. Barriers such as travel, caring responsibilities and accessibility should inform the design.

03 / Equipment and income

Protect the community purpose

Founder Iman Tadros confirms the intended arrangement: donated machinery and project equipment will belong to Virgin Mary Farm together with Risemeup, for the farm’s community purpose. This does not change ownership of the land.

The legal recipient and title, donor conditions, asset custody, maintenance and transfer or disposal rules will be recorded in written agreements with the Church and donors before equipment is accepted or delivered. The proposed income model would support operations, maintenance, education and community benefit; allocations and reporting need governance approval.

04 / Financial controls

Make support traceable

Proposed controls include a separate project ledger, documented purchasing and approvals, retained receipts, an asset register and regular expenditure and progress reports. Named oversight and reporting responsibilities must be agreed before funds are received.

Safeguarding, complaints and financial policies
