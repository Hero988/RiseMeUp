02 / A first stage to develop together

Restore water.
Start growing. Share skills.

A smaller first stage makes the next decisions concrete. This is a proposed scope for technical assessment, not an approved engineering design or a funded delivery programme.

Proposed scope

Assess the seven destroyed wells. Subject to technical findings, establish an initial water source through well rehabilitation or replacement, build new water storage, and install a complete pumping and irrigation system for an initial cultivation and training area. The growing area, crop plan and participant numbers will follow the water assessment and community consultation.

  1. 01

    Confirm the foundations

    Agree land-use terms, the legal project recipient and local responsibilities; confirm safe access; assess the seven destroyed wells and options for rehabilitation or replacement, including water availability and suitability.

    Evidence to proceed: dated assessment, well records and agreed responsibilities.

  2. 02

    Design and cost the first system

    Agree a viable water source, define the growing area, prepare well-restoration, new water-storage, pump and irrigation specifications, obtain comparable quotations and agree community participation and asset-custody arrangements.

    Evidence to proceed: technical design, itemised budget, operating plan and funding agreement.

  3. 03

    Install, test and hand over

    Once funding, access and secure storage are confirmed, procure compatible equipment, install the system, test its performance and train the named operators.

    Evidence to proceed: commissioning results, asset register, operator handover and maintenance schedule.

  4. 04

    Grow, teach and review

    Begin a supervised growing cycle and sustainable farming education. Record water use, crop performance, participation and costs, then decide with communities and partners whether to expand.

    Evidence for expansion: first-cycle review and an updated, costed plan.

Timing depends on safe access, assessment findings, written agreements, available funding and procurement. Delivery dates will be agreed after these dependencies are resolved.

A long-term ambition.

05 / Community benefit and accountability

Built with people.
Accountable to them.

The approach below is proposed for agreement with the Church, community representatives and development partners before implementation. These arrangements are not yet adopted operating policies.

Organisation status

Risemeup is a London-based initiative and is not currently registered in the UK. The appropriate legal structure and eligible recipient for any grant or donated equipment must be agreed before accepting project funding. UK charity or company registration is not claimed.

01 / Participation

Fair access to opportunity

Proposed selection criteria will consider household need, displacement, barriers to livelihoods and interest in farming or training. Criteria should be shared locally and applied transparently, with a way to raise concerns. Participation should not depend on religious observance.

02 / Community voice

Shape the project together

The proposed consultation process will include both the Coptic Orthodox and Nuba Mountains communities, with women, young people and displaced families helping to shape training, schedules and access. Barriers such as travel, caring responsibilities and accessibility should inform the design.

03 / Equipment and income

Protect the community purpose

The legal owner, custodian, maintenance responsibility and transfer or disposal rules for donated machinery still need written agreement. The proposed income model would support farm operations, maintenance, education and community benefit; allocations and reporting need governance approval.

04 / Financial controls

Make support traceable

Proposed controls include a separate project ledger, documented purchasing and approvals, retained receipts, an asset register and regular expenditure and progress reports. Named oversight and reporting responsibilities must be agreed before funds are received.

Safeguarding, complaints and financial policies

Adopted policy documents and responsible officers have not yet been published. Safeguarding, safe recruitment and conduct, confidential complaints, conflicts of interest and financial controls need to be agreed and put into use before activities begin. Approved versions and accessible reporting routes will then be published.

Iman can receive general questions about the proposed arrangements. A formal safeguarding or independent complaints route has not yet been confirmed for publication; please do not send sensitive case details through a general enquiry.

Reference for developing these arrangements: Misereor’s policies, guides and forms. This is a guidance reference, not a claim of partnership or endorsement.
