Virgin Mary Farm · For development partners

Rebuilding starts
with water.

Help restore water at Virgin Mary Farm so women and families can begin growing, learning and earning again.

Virgin Mary Farm in Omdurman is Risemeup’s main development project. It was destroyed during the war and is not operational. We are seeking water, irrigation and agricultural specialists to help assess the site and design a first growing and training area.

Proposed scope

Inspect the seven wells reported intact and specify replacements for the broken pumps. Design daytime solar pumping, a reservoir or suitable tank storage, filtration and controlled irrigation for an assessed growing and training area. Assess battery storage for agreed electrical loads outside daylight hours. Agree crops, participation and training with the communities.

  1. Confirm the foundations

    Agree land-use terms and local responsibilities, confirm safe access and assess the wells, water availability and quality.

    Evidence to proceed: dated assessment, well records and agreed responsibilities.

  2. Design and cost the first system

    Define the growing area and water system, obtain comparable quotations, and agree training, participation and equipment custody.

    Evidence to proceed: technical design, itemised budget, operating plan and funding agreement.

  3. Install, test and hand over

    Once funding, access and secure storage are confirmed, procure compatible equipment, install the system, test its performance and train the named operators.

    Evidence to proceed: commissioning results, asset register, operator handover and maintenance schedule.

  4. Grow, teach and review

    Begin a supervised growing cycle and sustainable farming education. Record water use, crop performance, participation and costs, then decide with communities and partners whether to expand.

    Evidence for expansion: first-cycle review and an updated, costed plan.

Timing depends on safe access, assessment findings, written agreements, available funding and procurement. Delivery dates will be agreed after these dependencies are resolved.

Women and girls at the heart of the project

A place to regain strength.
A future shaped by possibility.

We plan farming education and livelihood opportunities for women from the Coptic Orthodox, Nuba Mountains and Blue Nile communities affected by war, including survivors of sexual violence, widows and mothers raising children alone.

Dignity and independence

Women would gain practical skills, opportunities to earn an income and a voice in the farm’s development. The programme will be shaped with communities and partners as rebuilding progresses.

Opportunity for girls

For girls and young women, we plan education and skills opportunities that build confidence and widen future choices. Girls’ learning will be age-appropriate, supervised and designed to support their safety and continued schooling.

Developing this responsibly

Participation must be voluntary and must not require disclosure of violence or trauma. Consultation should address caring responsibilities, travel and accessibility. We welcome women’s organisations and qualified psychosocial partners to help develop support and referral arrangements. These services are not yet established; farming does not replace specialist care.

Training for everyone who works at the farm

Everyone working at the farm would receive training suited to their role, including sustainable farming, water management, safe equipment use and maintenance. Induction and continued learning would support each stage.

Dr. Eng. Khalid Salih brings university teaching experience from Berlin and expertise in renewable energy and workforce development. Our training plan will draw on his experience and the practical learning approach of the DIESALIHS Grand Renaissance Academy, alongside agricultural specialists and equipment suppliers. Course content, instructors and delivery arrangements will be developed as the farm is rebuilt.

Community benefit and accountability

Built with people.
Accountable to them.

The approach below is proposed for agreement with the Church, community representatives and development partners before implementation. These arrangements are not yet adopted operating policies.

Organisation status

Risemeup is a London-based initiative and is not currently registered in the UK. Before accepting project funding or equipment, the legal structure and eligible recipient must be agreed.

Participation

Fair access to opportunity

Proposed selection would consider household need, displacement, barriers to livelihoods and interest in farming or training. Criteria should be transparent, with a way to raise concerns. Participation should not depend on religious observance.

Community voice

Shape the project together

All three communities would help shape training, schedules and access, with women a priority. Consultation should address the needs of young people and displaced families, including travel, caring responsibilities and accessibility.

Equipment and income

Protect the community purpose

Founder Iman Tadros confirms the intended arrangement: donated machinery and project equipment will belong to Virgin Mary Farm together with Risemeup, for the farm’s community purpose. This does not change ownership of the land.

The legal recipient and title, donor conditions, asset custody, maintenance and transfer or disposal rules will be recorded in written agreements with the Church and donors before equipment is accepted or delivered. The proposed income model would support operations, maintenance, education and community benefit; allocations and reporting need governance approval.

Financial controls

Make support traceable

Proposed controls include a separate project ledger, documented purchasing and approvals, retained receipts, an asset register and regular expenditure and progress reports. Named oversight and reporting responsibilities must be agreed before funds are received.

Safeguarding, complaints and financial policies

Adopted policy documents and responsible officers have not yet been published. Safeguarding, safe recruitment and conduct, confidential complaints, conflicts of interest and financial controls need to be agreed and put into use before activities begin. Approved versions and accessible reporting routes will then be published.

Iman can receive general questions about the proposed arrangements. A formal safeguarding or independent complaints route has not yet been confirmed for publication; please do not send sensitive case details through a general enquiry.

Reference for developing these arrangements: Misereor’s policies, guides and forms. This is a guidance reference, not a claim of partnership or endorsement.
