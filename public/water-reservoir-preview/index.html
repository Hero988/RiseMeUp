Latest reported status · 27 October 2026

Seven existing wells reported intact. Pumps reported broken and requiring replacement. This update replaces the earlier account that the wells themselves were destroyed. A technical inspection and water tests are still needed.

See how the water could travel

A connected pathway: wells → reservoir → filter and controls → greenhouse drip irrigation.

Restore the water supply.

Inspect the wells, test water quality and sustainable yield, then specify suitable replacement pumps, controls and power. Pump selection depends on the operating water level, required flow and delivery pressure.

Store water for when it is needed.

Develop a lined reservoir or suitable tank storage, with protection against contamination and safe access. Its size, lining and any cover will follow the water assessment and crop needs.

Connect the greenhouses.

Use a main pipe with controlled branches to the planned greenhouses. Filtered drip lines would supply water close to plant roots, with each irrigation zone controlled separately.

Pumping and irrigation can run at different times

Store first. Irrigate when the crops need it.

The farm’s reported previous routine was pumping from 6 pm to 6 am, then irrigating from stored water. The proposed system could follow that routine if reliable night-time power is available.

If solar pumping is selected, daytime pumping into storage is another option to assess. The reservoir would separate pumping times from irrigation times. Gravity supply or an additional irrigation pump will depend on the site levels and pressure requirements.

What needs confirming before we request equipment?

Well condition and operating water levels; sustainable pumping rates and interaction between wells; water quality; greenhouse dimensions and crops; daily irrigation demand; storage capacity; site elevations; pipe routes; power availability; security, maintenance and a costed first-stage plan. The reported well depth of approximately 500 feet does not by itself determine pump size or water output.

The earlier barrel-filling account is historical information, not a current pump test or a confirmed supply for ten greenhouses.
