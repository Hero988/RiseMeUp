Read the five phases and funding approach

Water comes first. The technically assessed first growing area may be smaller than the indicative Phase 1 target. Safe access, water availability, secure storage, an agreed budget and trained operators must be established before production starts.

  1. 01

    Phase 1

    Start & produce

    20–30 hectaresIndicative first-phase area

    Assess the land and seven damaged wells, restore a viable water source and build new storage. Establish irrigation, suitable machinery, farmer groups and practical training before starting marketable crops.

    Planned benefit: A first harvest, practical skills and the beginnings of farm income.

  2. 02

    Phase 2

    Expand production

    50–60 hectaresProposed cumulative area

    Extend irrigation and cultivation, introduce suitable additional crops, expand training and improve transport, basic storage and post-harvest handling. Add machinery as the assessed workload grows.

    Planned benefit: More growing capacity, with any available surplus contributing to expansion.

  3. 03

    Phase 3

    Build commercial capacity

    80–90 hectaresProposed cumulative area

    Improve crop planning and rotation, mechanisation and organised harvesting. Develop cleaning, grading, storage and logistics, supported by direct relationships with buyers.

    Planned benefit: Stronger routes to market and better value from each harvest.

  4. 04

    Phase 4

    Add value & enterprise

    110 hectaresProposed cumulative area

    Develop packing facilities, a machinery workshop and spare-parts storage. Improve transport and buyer agreements, expand training, and assess circular farming enterprises, including biogas and organic fertiliser.

    Planned benefit: A broader range of agricultural work and potential sources of income.

  5. 05

    Phase 5

    Connect the whole farm

    130 hectaresTotal farm site · long-term framework

    Bring water, renewable energy, greenhouses, poultry, produce handling, machinery services and farming education into one coordinated farm. Agree long-term provision for production, maintenance, farmer income, reinvestment and community benefit.

    Planned benefit: A resilient farm supporting livelihoods and community recovery.

The proposed reinvestment approach

Grow. Earn. Reinvest.

We aim to begin generating income in the first productive phase and use available surplus to help fund the next. Future income and self-financing are ambitions, dependent on harvests, markets, costs and local conditions. Additional partner funding may be needed at every stage.

  1. Record harvest sales
  2. Cover actual operating costs
  3. Agree farmer income and maintenance reserves
  4. Reinvest available surplus in the farm and community

Based on the supplied five-phase planning illustration. All phase areas are indicative cumulative targets, not additional areas or current cultivation. The 130 hectares is the total site; a survey must establish cultivable land and space for buildings, access and water infrastructure. No delivery dates, returns or fixed income-allocation percentages are agreed. This recovery roadmap complements the four facility components of the original 2018 proposal.
