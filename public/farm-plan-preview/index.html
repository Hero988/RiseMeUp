Our full vision · Original 2018 proposal

What we plan
to build.

Four connected parts of one farm: renewable energy, food production, produce handling and poultry, supported by sustainable farming education and practical training across the whole project.

Historical proposal · The farm is not currently operational. These facilities describe our long-term vision, not the immediate rebuilding sequence. Our current proposed first stage focuses on assessment and restoring water access.

Phase 1 in the original proposal · Renewable energy

Biogas plant

Electricity · Gas · Fertiliser

We plan to build a biogas plant that turns agricultural residues, manure and other organic farm materials into useful energy and fertiliser.

The energy plan +
  • Generate electricity and gas to support farm operations.
  • Return the nutrient-rich material left after digestion to the land as fertiliser.
  • Make productive use of farm by-products and reduce dependence on purchased energy and fertiliser.

Why it matters: energy and soil inputs would be produced within the farm, supporting a more self-sufficient agricultural system.

02

Phase 2 in the original proposal · Horticulture

10 greenhouses to be built

Protected growing · Irrigation · Practical skills

We plan to design, build and equip ten modern commercial greenhouses, with irrigation systems for intensive fruit and vegetable production.

The greenhouse plan +
  • Create growing conditions suited to each crop and its stage of development.
  • Support off-season production and better protection from extreme weather, pests, weeds and disease.
  • Use water and other production resources efficiently while training community members in greenhouse farming.

Why it matters: more reliable crop production, agricultural jobs and skills that families can use to build livelihoods.

03

Phase 3 in the original proposal · Produce handling

Sorting & packaging facilities

Protect quality · Prepare produce · Reach markets

We plan to install sorting and packaging systems for fruit and vegetables, with automated processes that prepare produce for storage, distribution and sale.

The handling plan +
  • Clean, sort and grade harvested produce.
  • Pack, fill, seal and label products, with handling and palletising for distribution.
  • Protect produce quality and develop sales in local markets, with longer-term international opportunities subject to buyer and market requirements.

Why it matters: keeping more value from the harvest and creating work beyond growing, in packing, handling and distribution.

Learning across the whole farm

Sustainable farming education & training

We plan to make Virgin Mary Farm a place to learn as well as produce food. Sustainable farming education and hands-on training will help members of the Coptic Orthodox and Nuba Mountains communities build skills for employment, food security and lasting livelihoods.

Training for everyone who works at the farm

Our commitment is that everyone working at Virgin Mary Farm will receive training appropriate to their role. The planned programme will cover sustainable farming, water management, safe equipment use, maintenance and practical skills for earning a living, with induction and continued learning as the farm develops.

Dr. Eng. Khalid Salih brings university teaching experience from Berlin and expertise in renewable energy and workforce development. Our training plan will draw on his experience and the practical learning approach of the DIESALIHS Grand Renaissance Academy, alongside agricultural specialists and equipment suppliers. Course content, instructors and delivery arrangements will be developed as the farm is rebuilt.

Planned learning areas

Growing sustainably

Soil care, crop rotation, greenhouse growing and water-efficient irrigation, connecting sustainable farming principles with practical work on the land.

Using resources responsibly

Renewable energy, biogas and the productive use of organic farm by-products, helping learners understand how food production, energy and soil fertility work together.

Building farm livelihoods

Poultry care, harvesting, sorting and packaging, alongside basic farm management skills that support work and income opportunities.

We welcome agricultural educators, training institutions and development partners to help shape and deliver practical demonstrations, workshops and supervised learning. The programme will be developed with the communities as the farm is rebuilt.
